Your Great Work does not matter.

22-07-20265 min read (873 words)
Share:

“Create for the sake of creation, not for the sake of accomplishment, and you will be truly free from the shackles of normality.”

The Great Work#

The Great Work is a term from Alchemy and Hermeticism, referring to a singular ultimate work of the practitioner. Their Magnum Opus. The work that defines them and drives them to discover the hidden secrets of the universe.

We use the same mentality for our own creative works, striving for this ultimate goal, trying to be the very best.

But the rest of this philosophy seems to be missing in modern interpretations. Prevalent in spiritual alchemy is the idea that the work you do morphs you as much as you morph it. Throughout your journey of self-improvement, you will acquire all the answers. But knowledge comes at a cost.

You are an ingredient of the process, as much as the pencil and paper. You are a conduit for the creation. Logically the work leaves its mark on you, and by virtue of, makes you grow.

Odin and the World Tree#

The saga of Odin and the runes is a story about self sacrifice in order to achieve cosmic insight. Of course, read between the lines and don’t interpret any mythology as literal (you know who you are). I’ll summarize it, so we are on the same page -

Odin sacrificed himself to himself by hanging off the world tree (also the center of the world in Norse mythology) for 9 days, stabbed with his own spear. Through this offering, he acquired the knowledge of the runes.

Don’t hang yourself for esoteric teachings, nor magical alphabets, it won’t work.

Of course, this doesn’t make any sense, since a sacrifice means an offering to a higher being in order to receive something of theirs. It’s a deal. You can’t make deals with yourself.

Only from the self-transformation through the pain of the ritual, was he able to tap into the essence of the universe and draw knowledge. Pain and struggle have been considered sacred because they are a necessary ingredient for improvement. The pain is a conduit. A means to an end. A very similar in meaning is the saga of the All-father losing his eye -

Odin gave up one of his eyes, so he can drink from the Urd well of eternal cosmic knowledge in the roots of the world tree. Norse believed that Odin’s eye was a symbol of insight and understanding. It also stands for the sacrifice he made to get the cosmic enlightenment at the Urd well. Physical sight for cosmic insight.

Change, only as a result of sacrifice in order to achieve great goals. The hidden lesson in both sagas I gathered is something like -

You are your greatest work. Improving yourself through your work should be the goal. “Sacrifice” yourself through your work the best way you can and the result will be a better, ever-improving you.

Through you, does your work live, not the opposite.

My experience#

I’ve had a dilemma not so long ago. I make video games and it’s a long process. The average length of development of a mid-sized game is around 5 years, also known as a development cycle. Had to put a game of mine on a big hiatus, due to my day job at the time. Meanwhile, I picked up CAD modelling and 3D printing, and that was really fun. Obviously, the guilt of ditching my game for something else in my very limited free time (back then) was enormous. I was a game developer after all, that is what I’ve wanted to be most of my life. How dare I do something else, when I can work on my dream? Shameful.

I dug deeper and deeper into these feelings and after a great conversation with my partner (much love), I reached the culprit.

In my drive to live my dream, I’ve put myself in a neat little box called “game developer”. A social construct that made me forget that expressing myself was the whole point. Not making the games. Doing photography, drawing, writing, programming, CAD modelling, design - these are all expressions of myself onto the world, they are created with the same drive, energy, principles and personality. They are expressions of me and are defined by me. They do not define me. It doesn’t really matter what I start doing in the future, as long as I do it with the same “myself” as before.

There is no shame in exploring different activities, as they give me skills/knowledge that might be useful later on. When I picked up game development again, nothing was lost. It was all there, waiting for me, but I was a better version of myself.

The “correct” term is “maker”. Nothing else.

Being a creator is a lifelong journey and you should treat yourself as your Great Work because your mind is the only collection of everything you have made, experienced and thought. Don’t waste yourself in the process, like I started to (and reverting the damage done).

Treat yourself with dignity, drink water, take breaks, live to fight for your dreams another day. Experience the world in it’s entirety and you will be rewarded for the sacrifice.
