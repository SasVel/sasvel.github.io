Be the cartographer of your own path

Every creative journey starts with excitement. An incredible rush to create this new amazing thing, materializing before your very eyes. Sadly this feeling often withers away with time, when the project is big. What do you do then? What to do when the spark fades away?

Expectations vs reality#

Every skill requires constant effort for progress. Often that progress is not linear though. Some days you feel you make no progress whatsoever, others you are at 200% productivity and feel fantastic (often after an energy drink).

It would be so wonderful if we could just grind out some experience points and level up that skill forever. What makes improvement in videogames so satisfying, and improvement in real life so… off?

Expectations. In games we know exactly when the exp bar is going to fill up. We expect that filling up the bar equals to gaining a level - that feels good. Real life skills don’t have the luxury of a shiny UI menu with a progress bar.

Creative work rarely follows a predictable path. Progress is intangible, the experience curve is unknown. When our expectations are not met, we get worked up and either give up on the task altogether or force ourselves to match those expectations. Both of those are not a sustainable solution.

If we are always led by that moving goalpost, we would never be satisfied from our progress. Living in endless dread of not pushing hard enough, in spite of squeezing every last drop of willpower we can muster every single day. Because in our minds, with enough work done, making progress is a given. So not making progress would mean lacking behind on the effort put in. But there is no “enough work done”, it’s never enough.

Feeling sorry for yourself won’t really do any good, so how do we ease that endless stress? Or maybe even remove it altogether?

Same destination but a change of goal#

Not a revolutionary advice for sure, but worth mentioning nonetheless. Showing up everyday should be the goal. A target that is tangible is worth pursuing everything else is just promises. Just keep showing up. Don’t lie to yourself, prove it.

Of course, keep the dream alive and work steadily towards it, but remember that the work is the goal, because the destination is right around the corner and nowhere to be seen at the same time.

And a change of…destination?#

Every pursuit has a path necessary to get through until you arrive at the destination…and yours may be longer than expected. So make the journey a bit more exciting!

There is nothing wrong with working a bit out of your set scope to get that creative juice running again. Make a mini-game in your grand adventure, or make your hero party go on a little side quest. Break your own rules, fuck it!

Let your project change and grow with you as a maker. Of course, try not to go out of scope, but you yourself grow and develop as a person and your art is a reflection of that.

Small pieces#

Breaking tasks down makes then more manageable and makes progress possible, even when time is a constraint.

  • Break that goal into smaller goals. Then again. And again. Do one, and another, and another.
  • Journal those tasks down at the end of your work session and make a plan for the next.
  • Have a set start and end of the work session.

Journaling#

I do not mean task management, that is not the goal of the exercise. Your Kanban board, as great as it is will not cut it.

It’s about active acknowledgment of the work done and the work about to be done. Like a cartographer, map the current path and move forward.

This also helps me refine ideas and think about possible bugs I’ve done, it’s like a self made peer review.

Sometimes I journal on paper, but if I want to keep my thoughts and use them for something else I use Obsidian. It’s the best tool I’ve used for note taking and mapping out ideas, since it’s really simple, but incredibly extensible with plugins and templates. The app also uses markdown, which is a standard format for writing structured text, so you are not closed in any ecosystem, like with Notion, for example.

Reuse, recycle, salvage#

I don’t believe abandoning a project is hardly ever the solution. Transform or salvage it if possible. Value your own work and let it see the light of day, even if it changes in the process, or you miss every deadline. No one is going to care after 20 years whether you hit your deadline in time, but they might care about that thing you created.

If that is not an option, salvage the ideas behind it at least. The concept, a part of the narrative, the characters or pieces of code. Anything can be used as pieces in your next creative endeavor.

Summary#

Creative work rarely follows a predictable path, and progress won’t always feel visible. When the initial spark fades, focus on showing up, breaking the work into manageable steps, and making the journey enjoyable again.

Let the project evolve with you, take time to reflect on what you’ve done, and remember that even an unfinished project can leave behind ideas worth carrying forward.
